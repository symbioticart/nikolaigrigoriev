The Core Principle

Read1 min

Our task is not sim­ply to adapt to the de­vel­op­ment of ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence, but to build a per­son­al and eco­nom­ic sys­tem that grows stronger be­cause of tech­no­log­i­cal change, in­clud­ing un­ex­pect­ed change.

The development of AI is steadi­ly low­er­ing the cost of in­tel­lec­tu­al work: pro­gram­ming, analy­sis, de­sign, con­tent pro­duc­tion and de­ci­sion-mak­ing.

This changes how eco­nom­ic value is dis­trib­uted. Some skills and as­sets lose value, oth­ers find new ap­pli­ca­tions, and still oth­ers be­come rel­a­tive­ly scarcer.

The strat­e­gy is to use AI to de­vel­op our own ca­pa­bil­i­ties and to ac­cu­mu­late as­sets whose value does not dis­ap­pear as tech­nol­o­gy con­tin­ues to im­prove.

Here we need to dis­tin­guish be­tween three states:

  • AI-efficient: AI makes an ac­tiv­i­ty more ef­fi­cient.
  • AI-resistant: an ac­tiv­i­ty re­tains its value as AI de­vel­ops.
  • AI-antifragile: tech­no­log­i­cal change cre­ates ad­di­tion­al op­por­tu­ni­ties to grow cap­i­tal, with lim­it­ed po­ten­tial loss­es.

Our goal: the third state.

Part I1. Personal Antifragility Architecture

Read2 min

The sys­tem con­sists of three in­ter­con­nect­ed lev­els.

Antifragile Human

AI-antifragile: technological change creates additional opportunities to grow capital, with limited potential losses.

The first level: de­vel­op­ing a per­son who can re­main in­de­pen­dent and make use of tech­no­log­i­cal un­cer­tain­ty.

This re­quires ac­cu­mu­lat­ing six kinds of per­son­al cap­i­tal.

Intellectual capital: sys­tems think­ing, crit­i­cal judg­ment, the abil­i­ty to frame prob­lems, form hy­pothe­ses and make de­ci­sions.

Creative capital: taste, orig­i­nal­i­ty, the abil­i­ty to con­nect dif­fer­ent fields of knowl­edge and cre­ate new con­cepts.

Social capital: trust, rep­u­ta­tion, pro­fes­sion­al net­works, com­mu­ni­ties and the abil­i­ty to or­ga­nize col­lec­tive work.

Physical capital: health, en­er­gy, cog­ni­tive per­for­mance and the length of ac­tive life.

Economic capital: liq­uid­i­ty, own­er­ship, di­ver­si­fied in­come and fi­nan­cial in­de­pen­dence.

Existential capital: inner au­ton­o­my, sta­ble val­ues and an iden­ti­ty that does not de­pend on any par­tic­u­lar pro­fes­sion.

The key professional transformation: from a spe­cial­ist who car­ries out tasks to an ar­chi­tect of so­lu­tions who choos­es the prob­lems, brings to­geth­er peo­ple, tech­nol­o­gy and cap­i­tal, or­ga­nizes ex­per­i­ments and owns part of the value cre­at­ed.

Human an­tifragili­ty lies not in a uni­ver­sal abil­i­ty to com­pete with AI, but in the ca­pac­i­ty to change one’s ways of work­ing with­out los­ing in­de­pen­dence or ac­cess to new op­por­tu­ni­ties.

Antifragile Capital

The sec­ond level: ac­cu­mu­lat­ing eco­nom­ic as­sets that hold their value and may ben­e­fit from the fur­ther de­vel­op­ment of AI.

The core principle: buy, cre­ate or con­trol what be­comes more valu­able as in­tel­li­gence be­comes cheap­er.

But scarci­ty alone is not enough.

The core principle: buy, create or control what becomes more valuable as intelligence becomes cheaper.

We need to dis­tin­guish be­tween ir­re­pro­ducibil­i­ty and ir­re­place­abil­i­ty.

For ex­am­ple, the rights to a work of art are legal­ly pro­tect­ed. Yet a more ca­pa­ble AI can cre­ate an at­trac­tive al­ter­na­tive and re­duce de­mand for the orig­i­nal.

That is why as­sets are es­pe­cial­ly in­ter­est­ing when their value is de­ter­mined not only by the qual­i­ty of the prod­uct but also by char­ac­ter­is­tics that can­not be re­pro­duced au­to­mat­i­cal­ly.

Such char­ac­ter­is­tics may in­clude a unique phys­i­cal lo­ca­tion, his­tor­i­cal au­then­tic­i­ty, real human pres­ence, trust, so­cial recog­ni­tion, proven achieve­ment, ac­cess to in­fra­struc­ture or legal au­thor­i­ty.

Even an asset that is fun­da­men­tal­ly ir­re­pro­ducible does not guar­an­tee re­turns. One must prove that there is sol­vent de­mand and that the owner is able to cap­ture eco­nom­ic rent.

AI Leverage

The third level: using ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence to the fullest to de­vel­op the first two.

Key principle: AI builds. You own.

AI should carry out a grow­ing share of re­search, an­a­lyt­i­cal, pro­duc­tion and op­er­a­tional work.

This re­quires a per­son­al in­tel­li­gence in­fra­struc­ture of four in­ter­con­nect­ed com­po­nents.

Memory: ac­cu­mu­lat­ing your own knowl­edge, de­ci­sions, re­search, con­tacts and re­sults in ac­ces­si­ble, ex­portable for­mats.

Intelligence: using dif­fer­ent mod­els to ex­plore op­por­tu­ni­ties, an­a­lyze de­ci­sions, test hy­pothe­ses and find er­rors.

Execution: del­e­gat­ing de­vel­op­ment, re­search and re­cur­ring process­es to agents while keep­ing con­trol of sig­nif­i­cant de­ci­sions.

Learning: sys­tem­at­i­cal­ly com­par­ing fore­casts with ac­tu­al re­sults and re­fin­ing your own model of the world.

The aim is not for AI to make every de­ci­sion for a per­son. The aim is to raise the qual­i­ty and speed of human think­ing, as well as the abil­i­ty to turn de­ci­sions into re­al­i­ty.

Key principle: AI builds. You own.

AI is be­com­ing a uni­ver­sal re­source of pro­duc­tion. A per­son seeks to own the re­sults, re­la­tion­ships, as­sets and sys­tems that cre­ate long-term value.

Part II2. Scarcity Migration Investing

Read1 min

One of the fun­da­men­tal ef­fects of AI: the mi­gra­tion of eco­nom­ic scarci­ty.

When technology radically cheap­ens one fac­tor of pro­duc­tion, the next one be­comes the con­straint.

If pro­gram­ming gets cheap­er, ac­cess to cus­tomers, in­fra­struc­ture and real-world data may be­come more im­por­tant.

If con­tent pro­duc­tion be­comes prac­ti­cal­ly free, value may shift to trust, recog­ni­tion and au­di­ence at­ten­tion.

If the de­sign of phys­i­cal ob­jects is au­to­mat­ed, the con­straints may be­come pro­duc­tion ca­pac­i­ty, ma­te­ri­als, per­mits and ac­cess to sites.

Hence the in­vest­ment strat­e­gy: an­tic­i­pate where scarci­ty will mi­grate and gain con­trol of emerg­ing sources of eco­nom­ic rent be­fore the mar­ket fully reprices them.

It is not nec­es­sary to ac­quire the asset it­self.

But control over scarcity does not guarantee profit.

One can ob­tain eq­ui­ty stakes, li­cens­es, ex­clu­sive rights, man­age­ment con­tracts or op­tions on fu­ture deals.

But con­trol over scarci­ty does not guar­an­tee prof­it. One has to un­der­stand who will pay, why the hold­er of that par­tic­u­lar right will get the money, and how quick­ly com­peti­tors can re­move the con­straint.

Sit­u­a­tions are es­pe­cial­ly in­ter­est­ing when AI changes de­mand faster than phys­i­cal or in­sti­tu­tion­al sys­tems can ad­just sup­ply.

Part III3. The Economics of Countertrends

Read1 min

The de­vel­op­ment of AI may cre­ate de­mand not only for new tech­nolo­gies but also for the qual­i­ties op­po­site to them.

These countertrends are not guaranteed forecasts. They have to be confirmed by the behavior of buyers.

The growth of syn­thet­ic con­tent may raise the value of ver­i­fied au­then­tic­i­ty.

The spread of vir­tu­al in­ter­ac­tion may make real human pres­ence more at­trac­tive.

The au­toma­tion of out­put may in­crease the sym­bol­ic value of per­son­al ef­fort and crafts­man­ship.

An abun­dance of high-qual­i­ty prod­ucts may strength­en the role of trust, sta­tus and so­cial recog­ni­tion.

Au­to­mat­ed de­ci­sion-mak­ing may in­crease the need for in­de­pen­dent ver­i­fi­ca­tion, au­thor­i­ty and ac­count­abil­i­ty for real-world con­se­quences.

These coun­tertrends are not guar­an­teed fore­casts. They have to be con­firmed by the be­hav­ior of buy­ers.

But they set an im­por­tant di­rec­tion for the search: study­ing human needs that can­not be fully met sim­ply by adding more com­put­ing power.

Pri­or­i­ty goes to re­sources that are hard to re­pro­duce, hard to re­place eco­nom­i­cal­ly, and whose value grows as AI spreads.

Part IV4. Optionality and Positive Asymmetry

Read1 min

No asset guar­an­tees a win under every tech­no­log­i­cal sce­nario.

That is why an­tifragili­ty must be a prop­er­ty not only of an in­di­vid­ual com­pa­ny but of the whole sys­tem of de­ci­sion-mak­ing and cap­i­tal al­lo­ca­tion.

We should seek the right to take ad­van­tage of a fa­vor­able fu­ture with­out the oblig­a­tion to fi­nance an un­fa­vor­able one.

In prac­tice this means small ex­per­i­ments, lim­it­ed ini­tial com­mit­ments, exit terms de­fined in ad­vance, the ac­qui­si­tion of op­tions, and in­creas­ing in­vest­ment grad­u­al­ly, only after hy­pothe­ses are con­firmed.

We should seek the right to take advantage of a favorable future without the obligation to finance an unfavorable one.

This is where the Bar­bell strat­e­gy ap­plies.

One part of per­son­al re­sources pro­vides sta­bil­i­ty: health, liq­uid­i­ty, re­li­able in­come and so­cial cap­i­tal.

The other goes to asym­met­ric op­por­tu­ni­ties, where the po­ten­tial gain sub­stan­tial­ly ex­ceeds the pos­si­ble loss­es.

This makes it pos­si­ble to com­bine fi­nan­cial se­cu­ri­ty with ex­per­i­men­ta­tion and not de­pend on any sin­gle fore­cast being right.

Part V5. Twelve Mandatory Criteria of an AI-Antifragile Business

Read2 min

Every busi­ness hy­poth­e­sis must be test­ed against all twelve re­quire­ments.

All criteria are mandatory. Strong results on some points do not compensate for failing others.

1. Positive dependence on the development of AI. As AI im­proves and gets cheap­er, the com­pa­ny’s eco­nom­ic op­por­tu­ni­ties grow. Sim­ply cut­ting op­er­at­ing costs is not enough.

2. Advantage over competitors. With equal ac­cess to AI mod­els, the com­pa­ny’s com­pet­i­tive ad­van­tage is pre­served or strength­ened.

3. Resilience to technological paradigm shifts. The busi­ness does not crit­i­cal­ly de­pend on a spe­cif­ic AI model, plat­form or tech­no­log­i­cal ar­chi­tec­ture. Rad­i­cal change does not au­to­mat­i­cal­ly de­stroy its core source of value.

4. Positive asymmetry under uncertainty. Un­ex­pect­ed tech­no­log­i­cal change can pro­duce dis­pro­por­tion­ate­ly large gains with lim­it­ed po­ten­tial loss­es.

5. A protected economic advantage. The com­pa­ny con­trols a re­source or sys­tem that a com­peti­tor can­not quick­ly re­pro­duce, even with prac­ti­cal­ly free in­tel­li­gence.

6. Ownership of the value created. The founders own as­sets, eq­ui­ty, IP or eco­nom­ic rights that give them a share in fu­ture prof­its. The busi­ness is not re­duced to sell­ing their own time.

Each point is given a status: confirmed, unconfirmed or refuted.

7. Compounding effect. Each suc­cess­ful cycle of ac­tiv­i­ty strength­ens the com­pet­i­tive po­si­tion, in­creas­es the value of as­sets or ex­pands the com­pa­ny’s fu­ture op­por­tu­ni­ties.

8. Capture of economic rent. There is a spe­cif­ic, pro­tect­ed mech­a­nism that lets the com­pa­ny re­tain the value it cre­ates rather than pass it on to com­peti­tors, cus­tomers, sup­pli­ers or AI plat­forms.

9. Real, solvent demand. The com­pa­ny solves a prob­lem that cus­tomers are will­ing to pay for. Its eco­nom­ics do not rest sole­ly on ex­pec­ta­tions that asset val­ues will rise.

10. Disproportionate scaling. Rev­enue, prof­it or asset value can grow sub­stan­tial­ly faster than costs, head­count and the founders’ work­ing time.

11. Self-funded launch for under €20,000. The core hy­poth­e­sis can be test­ed, the first valu­able asset cre­at­ed or the first pay­ing cus­tomer won with­out manda­to­ry out­side fund­ing.

12. Limited risk and high optionality. A failed ex­per­i­ment does not de­stroy cap­i­tal. Com­mit­ments are con­trolled, the project can be stopped, and there are al­ter­na­tive ways to use the re­sults.

All criteria are manda­to­ry. Strong re­sults on some points do not com­pen­sate for fail­ing oth­ers.

Each point is given a sta­tus: con­firmed, un­con­firmed or re­fut­ed.

A busi­ness pass­es the se­lec­tion only once all twelve con­di­tions are con­firmed.

Three Stress Tests

In ad­di­tion, every model must pass three tests.

If a business fails even one test, it must be reworked or excluded.

First: AI has be­come a hun­dred times more pow­er­ful and cheap­er. Why does the value of our busi­ness in­crease rather than de­crease?

Second: com­peti­tors have gained ac­cess to the same AI. Why can they not re­pro­duce our ad­van­tage and wipe out our mar­gin?

Third: tech­nol­o­gy has de­vel­oped along a com­plete­ly un­ex­pect­ed path. Which as­sets sur­vive, which op­por­tu­ni­ties ap­pear, and how lim­it­ed are the loss­es?

If a busi­ness fails even one test, it must be re­worked or ex­clud­ed.

Part VI6. Practical Strategy

Read1 min

Ini­tial cap­i­tal is used above all to find and test asym­met­ric op­por­tu­ni­ties, not for large ir­re­versible in­vest­ments.

The working cycle: re­search struc­tur­al change, iden­ti­fy new de­mand or scarci­ty, form a busi­ness hy­poth­e­sis, test it against the twelve cri­te­ria, run a small mar­ket ex­per­i­ment, se­cure the eco­nom­ic rights, and then scale.

Pri­or­i­ty goes to projects where one can first con­firm de­mand or ob­tain the right to an op­por­tu­ni­ty, and only then raise cap­i­tal to pur­sue it.

AI-resistant: an activity retains its value as AI develops.

The main measures of progress: the share of in­come not di­rect­ly tied to per­son­al time; the value of the eco­nom­ic as­sets under con­trol; the qual­i­ty and speed of test­ing hy­pothe­ses; the amount of risk per ex­per­i­ment; the sys­tem’s abil­i­ty to pre­serve and ex­pand its op­tions as tech­nol­o­gy changes.

Over a ten-year hori­zon, the task is not nec­es­sar­i­ly to build one large com­pa­ny, but to as­sem­ble a port­fo­lio of in­de­pen­dent as­sets and busi­ness sys­tems with pos­i­tive asym­me­try.

Part VII7. Personal Strategy

Read1 min

Ul­ti­mate­ly, eco­nom­ic an­tifragili­ty should serve per­son­al free­dom.

The aim is to re­duce de­pen­dence on any par­tic­u­lar pro­fes­sion, em­ploy­er, tech­nol­o­gy plat­form, and on the need to keep sell­ing one’s own time.

In­stead of try­ing to keep in­di­vid­ual pro­fes­sion­al skills rel­e­vant for­ev­er, one should de­vel­op the abil­i­ty to un­der­stand change, choose di­rec­tions, or­ga­nize re­sources and own the re­sults.

The goal: greater freedom, more capital and the ability to use uncertainty as a source of new opportunities.

At the same time, health, re­la­tion­ships, cre­ativ­i­ty and per­son­al ex­pe­ri­ence should not be treat­ed mere­ly as in­stru­ments of eco­nom­ic pro­duc­tiv­i­ty. They have value in their own right and give us the free­dom to choose our own goals.

Conclusion

Read1 min

Per­son­al An­tifragili­ty Ar­chi­tec­ture brings to­geth­er three in­ter­con­nect­ed process­es: human de­vel­op­ment, the ac­cu­mu­la­tion of cap­i­tal, and the use of AI as a uni­ver­sal tool for ex­pand­ing pos­si­bil­i­ties.

The main principle: not to com­pete with an ac­cel­er­at­ing ar­ti­fi­cial in­tel­li­gence at pro­duc­ing what it does ever bet­ter and more cheap­ly, but to use its de­vel­op­ment to ac­quire and cre­ate eco­nom­i­cal­ly valu­able as­sets that are hard to re­pro­duce or re­place.

At the same time, we should not bet every­thing on a sin­gle fore­cast of the fu­ture. We need a port­fo­lio of op­por­tu­ni­ties with lim­it­ed risk, ca­pa­ble of win­ning under dif­fer­ent tech­no­log­i­cal sce­nar­ios.

The ul­ti­mate goal is not max­i­mum au­toma­tion, nor even max­i­mum in­come.

The goal: greater free­dom, more cap­i­tal and the abil­i­ty to use un­cer­tain­ty as a source of new op­por­tu­ni­ties.
