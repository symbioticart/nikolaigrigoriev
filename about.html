Nikolai Grigoriev

I make a life visible, so it can be lived as a work of art. Attention is what death sharpens: you see what a day is worth once you know they run out. I take one life — the record a body keeps without being asked — and turn it, slowly, into an image. Many of these lives collect into one piece for a museum. The work holds up no live mirror: it gives your day back only after the day has closed, so there is nothing to perform for. Medicine lengthens the body. I work on the part of a life that art can reach — its felt time, and the sense of having lived it.

I have been afraid of dying since I was a child. I refused to make that fear my subject and made its opposite my subject instead. Death ends a life, but it is also what makes a life worth composing: the pressure that turns ordinary days into something you would want to have lived on purpose. So I work on living rather than on dying, with death behind me, sharpening the attention.

I take a single life as material, mine first, because I do not ask of a work what I have not lived inside it. A body records itself while you are busy being alive. The work asks nothing of you, so what it takes is a life lived rather than one staged for it. No single life is the whole of the work, though. Each one feeds a larger body: the lives kept in a piece accrue into a collective form made to stand in a museum, a common portrait of many people living with attention.

Under all of it is a refusal to end. The work slows perception, so a day is felt for longer than it lasted. It keeps every day it has lived, so the life survives as a record. It is built to outlast the body, and to go on after me from the life of a stranger I will never meet. I came to art late and built its language myself. I do not want to leave objects. I want to leave a way of living.

Biography

Nikolai Grigoriev (b. 1987, Moscow) is a conceptual artist. He lives and works in Barcelona.
